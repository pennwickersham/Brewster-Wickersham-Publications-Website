The Resilient Path

Book

Managing Life with Chronic Pain · Pendleton B. Wickersham, MD, and Louise Locker, LCSW

You have been told to manage your pain. Nobody has told you how to live with it. That gap is where most people living with chronic pain spend years — the diagnosis arrives, the prescriptions start, the appointments stack up, and the daily work of actually living with the condition falls to you.

Written from experience, guided by expertise.

Dr. Wickersham is a practicing rheumatologist who has spent his career in that gap with his patients, and who has lived with ankylosing spondylitis since he was 22. This book is what he wishes he had time to say in every appointment. Written for people with rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, fibromyalgia, osteoarthritis, and other autoimmune and chronic pain conditions.

Grounded in the biopsychosocial model, it shows how your body, your mind, and the life around you all shape how much pain you feel — and how each one offers a way to turn the volume down. Not a to-do list to finish, but a way of living well with pain, one step at a time.

The Resilient Path Workbook

Workbook

Pendleton B. Wickersham, MD, and Louise Locker, LCSW

Reading about managing chronic pain and actually managing it are two different things. The book helps you understand; the workbook is where the lessons take root and become yours. Every chapter matches a chapter of the book, so you are never left wondering which exercise goes with what you just read.

Every exercise is approached through three lenses — patience, self-acceptance, and honesty — which turn a list of activities into a way of being.

  • Guided reflections that surface the patterns you have not noticed
  • Trackers for pain, fatigue, sleep, and flares — turning scattered impressions into information you can bring to your doctor
  • Pacing plans built around your real energy, not an ideal day
  • Scripts for the hard conversations with family, employers, and clinicians
  • Flare planning you do while you are well, so the decisions are already made when you are not

The pages are yours to write on. There are no right answers and nothing to score.

Available now

iOS and Android

A flare tracker first. Flares are the thing that actually changes your treatment, and they are the hardest thing to describe accurately eight weeks later at an appointment. RheumCompanion captures one while it is happening — severity, duration, what preceded it — and turns a season of scattered impressions into a record your rheumatologist can read in a minute.

  • Flare tracking — log a flare as it happens, with severity and duration, while the details are still accurate
  • Daily symptoms and joint pain — pain, fatigue, and stiffness in a few taps, on the days you can manage it
  • Diet and symptom correlation — see what lines up with your flares over enough days to mean something
  • Medications and biologic infusion dates — your regimen and schedule alongside the symptom record, so changes and flares sit on the same timeline
  • Lab history — results kept together, where a trend is visible
  • Appointment summary — export a 30-day report to hand your care team at the start of a visit
  • Disease encyclopedia and medication reference — 13 conditions and 7 drug classes, drawn from FDA package inserts and PubMed research
  • RheumBot, interaction checker, and clinical trial finder — sourced answers, interactions grouped by severity, and what is recruiting for your condition

Louise Locker, LCSW

Counselor and community leader

Louise Locker has spent most of her life noticing what people need and quietly building it.

At nineteen, wanting to find a little girl to whom to give her childhood dolls, she persuaded the post office to let her read unopened letters to Santa (“Can’t you just think of me as Santa?”). Among them was a letter from a child explaining that Santa had never come only because they had never written. She kept reading and found 13 families who would have nothing for Christmas. Rather than hand the letters to a charity, she acted on a question she had long asked: what would the world be like if everyone acted on the goodness inside them? She approached strangers at a diner counter, and they helped. That beginning eventually became the Elf Louise Christmas Project, an all-volunteer San Antonio effort that has since delivered more than 1.5 million presents to children in need. She led the project for fifty years, during which she shared her message of compassion and giving as a guest on national television and radio shows.

As a licensed counselor and life coach, Louise has helped people through physical pain, chronic illness, loss, and setbacks. She also knows pain from the inside, living with inflammatory osteoarthritis, and much of what she teaches about hope, steadiness, and pacing, she learned first in her own life.

In 2024 the National Association of Social Workers named her a Social Work Pioneer, honoring her as an advocate for resilience.

With rheumatologist Dr. Pendleton Wickersham, she co-authored Managing Life with Chronic Pain: The Resilient Path Workbook. The workbook allows the practical application of the recommendations found in the accompanying book.

She lives in San Antonio, still practicing hope one steady step at a time, and still imagining and creating what can be.
